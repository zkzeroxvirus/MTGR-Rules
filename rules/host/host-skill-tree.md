# Host Skill Tree

Talent Points may be allocated only outside an active or pending-verification run. During the playtest, a Host may refund and reallocate all points at no cost.

Each node costs 1 Talent Point. Tier I requires Rank 2. Tier II requires Rank 4 and the preceding node in that branch. Tier III requires Rank 7 and the preceding Tier II node. A Host may invest across multiple branches.

| Branch | Tier I | Tier II | Tier III |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dominion | Seal the Combo | Answer the Answer | Last Word - STUB |
| Torment | Cruel Choice | Forced Exposure | Marked for Death - STUB |
| Control | Strip the Shield | Grave Interdict | Divide and Conquer - STUB |

Tier III nodes are visible destinations but are inactive. They cannot be purchased, granted, equipped, or counted as active unlocks until their effects are approved.

Learning a node adds its Command to the Host's available library. It does not equip the Command or add a Command Slot. If a respec removes an equipped Command, the saved loadout becomes invalid and the Host Profile Tool replaces the invalid entry before the next run locks.

## Branch identities

Every proposed Command must have one primary branch identity. A Command may touch an adjacent mechanic, but its main purpose, timing, and counterplay must pass that branch's identity test.

### Dominion - rule the stack

**Fantasy:** The Host is the final authority over how the encounter unfolds. Dominion protects the Host's plan, interrupts coordinated combo turns, and wins narrow contests on the stack.

**Player experience:** Precise, visible intervention. Players should understand what action drew the Host's response and should still be able to sequence around it, bait it, or answer it.

**Primary mechanics:** Countering spells or abilities; changing or restricting stack interaction; protecting Host spells and abilities from player interaction; temporarily suppressing a source after its ability is answered; responding to repeated or coordinated actions.

**Preferred timing:** Reactions while a spell or ability is on the stack. Dominion should usually require a specific legal target and a clearly defined trigger window.

**Dominion is not:** Unconditional removal, broad resource taxation, random punishment, permanent silence, or a rule that prevents players from meaningfully using the stack for the rest of the encounter.

**Identity test:** If the Command's main purpose is to decide whether, how, or against what a spell or ability resolves, it is Dominion.

### Torment - make every choice hurt

**Fantasy:** The Host weaponizes ambition, fear, and incomplete information. Torment does not simply say no; it offers a consequential choice, exposes plans, or makes a player decide which resource they can afford to lose.

**Player experience:** Pressure with agency. The consequence may be severe, but the affected player should normally choose, anticipate, or influence the outcome.

**Primary mechanics:** Life payments; discard or reveal choices; temporary taxes tied to a declared behavior; punisher choices; marking a player or card for a later consequence; rewards for the Host when a player accepts a risk; effects that become stronger when players overextend.

**Preferred timing:** Triggered reactions to recognizable behavior or priority actions during a clear Host window. Hidden information may be revealed, but hidden rules and surprise bookkeeping should be avoided.

**Torment is not:** Random discard, unavoidable life loss without a decision or trigger, direct player elimination, permanent hand denial, or punishment for ordinary participation with no practical alternative.

**Identity test:** If the Command's main purpose is to force a player to choose how they pay for continuing their plan, it is Torment.

### Control - reshape the battlefield

**Fantasy:** The Host dictates where players may safely act. Control strips protections, closes zones, divides coordinated boards, and creates temporary openings for the encounter to fight back.

**Player experience:** Board-state disruption that can be read and played through. Control should create a window of vulnerability or reposition the fight rather than erase a player's game plan without recourse.

**Primary mechanics:** Removing hexproof, indestructible, ward, or other protections; tapping or temporarily disabling permanents; graveyard interdiction; combat restrictions; temporary zone-access denial; separating, goading, or limiting groups of permanents; changing legal targets or combat roles when the effect is primarily about the battlefield.

**Preferred timing:** Host priority while the stack is empty, the beginning of a phase, or a reaction to a battlefield event. Durations should normally end at end of turn, the Host's next turn, or encounter end.

**Control is not:** Generic counterspells, unrestricted permanent destruction or exile, permanent theft, mass land denial, repeated turn denial, or a hard lock that prevents players from advancing the encounter.

**Identity test:** If the Command's main purpose is to change what permanents, zones, or combatants can do after they exist, it is Control.

## Shared Command design guardrails

Every branch Command should satisfy all of the following:

1. It creates a tactical sidegrade, not a permanent increase to Host life, damage, cards, land drops, Authority, or Command Slots.
2. Its trigger and legal targets can be stated before the Host spends Authority.
3. Its board state and duration can be tracked without private notes or repeated memory checks.
4. Players retain at least one meaningful form of counterplay: sequencing, payment, target protection, removal, baiting, or waiting out a duration.
5. It resolves in less time than the player action it is answering whenever practical.
6. It does not duplicate another branch's defining job more efficiently than that branch.
7. Its strongest use is exciting once per encounter and would not be acceptable as a repeatable action.

## Command proposal template

Dev-team proposals should include:

- **Name**
- **Branch and intended node**
- **Fantasy sentence:** what the Host appears to do
- **Timing:** priority action or exact reaction window
- **Cost and limit:** normally 1 Authority and once per encounter
- **Rules text:** targets, choices, duration, and zone changes
- **Branch identity test:** one sentence explaining why it belongs here
- **Counterplay:** how players can anticipate, answer, or mitigate it
- **Tracking:** token, marker, card, or no persistent tracking
- **Abuse check:** the strongest likely interaction and why it remains acceptable

## Expansion shape under review

The current three-node rows are the playtest skeleton, not the final tree size. The intended long-term library may contain approximately 30 powers from three acquisition sources:

- automatic Rank unlocks;
- Talent Point purchases in Dominion, Torment, and Control;
- achievement-based unlocks.

A candidate full branch contains a linear foundation, one mutually exclusive specialization fork, a mastery node, and a capstone. This expansion shape is design guidance only; it does not create new nodes, Rank gates, or Commands until their individual text is approved.
